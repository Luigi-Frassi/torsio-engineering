What it calculates

  • Friction joints and flange couplings: shear force or torque on a bolt circle → required preload, number of bolts, size and strength class (4.6 to 12.9).
  • Tightening torque with thread friction and under-head friction, and the von Mises check of the bolt during tightening.
  • External axial load: bolt and member stiffness, load increase in the bolt, joint separation, optimal preload, tightening diagram.
  • Check from a given tightening torque: the load the joint can transmit.
  • Power screws (square, trapezoidal, ISO thread): torque, efficiency, self-locking.

Method

The friction joint transmits the load through the friction between the plates: the preload comes from the required safety factor, the bolt size from the allowable stress on the resisting area, and the tightening torque from the thread geometry with the friction under the head.

Worked example

Exam problem of 2 February 2021: flange coupling transmitting a torque by friction.
TorqueMt = 32 kN·m on a bolt circle of 210 mm
Frictionf = 0.14 between the flanges and in the thread, 1 friction surface
Safety factor and classX = 1.5, class 12.9
Bolt sizeM30 (Ares = 452 mm²); at most 14 fit on the circle
Number of bolts10 × M30 12.9
Preload per bolt326.5 kN
Tightening torque1769 N·m per bolt
Bolt safety factor1.50: 10.02 bolts are needed, rounded down to 10 because the factor drops by less than 0.5 %, as in the hand solution

Limits and next step

Preliminary sizing: bolt fatigue and thermal effects are not included. For the final design use VDI 2230 and the friction values of your bolts.

Questions

Which friction coefficient should I use?
Use the values of your parts if you have them. As an order of magnitude, lightly oiled steel threads are often around 0.10–0.16 and untreated dry steel plates around 0.2; the exam example uses 0.14.
Does it handle an external axial load?
Yes: it computes the bolt and member stiffness, how much of the load goes into the bolt, whether the joint opens, and the optimal preload, with the tightening diagram.
Can I start from the torque on a flange coupling?
Yes: enter the torque and the bolt circle diameter, it converts them into the force on the bolts and checks how many bolts fit on the circle.
Is it free?
Yes, free and without sign-up. Everything runs in your browser.
