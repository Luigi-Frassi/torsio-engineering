Spur and helical gear calculator

From power, speed and ratio to the module, the number of teeth and the face width: Hertz contact pressure, Lewis bending check and the maximum power of an existing pair, for spur and helical gears, with the calculation report.

What it calculates

  • Design of a gear pair from power and speed (or torque) and the ratio: standard module, teeth and face width at the Hertz limit.
  • Automatic search of the best tooth combinations for a target ratio, with a tolerance, or for a given centre distance (the helix angle is adjusted to fit it).
  • Lewis bending stress with the Lewis factor y from the course chart; for helical gears the factors Φ, Ψ and Γₜ.
  • Maximum power an existing pair can transmit (Hertz and Lewis), from module, face width and teeth.
  • Profile shift of the pinion, and the results passed on request to the shaft calculator.

Method

The face width comes from the Hertz contact pressure between the two pitch cylinders at the pitch point; the tooth root is then checked in bending with Lewis. For helical gears the course factors Φ (Hertz), Ψ (Lewis) and Γₜ (curvature) correct the spur formulas.

Worked example

Exam problem of 7 January 2019, second stage of a coaxial reducer: helical pair on a 182 mm centre distance.
Power and speed50 kW at 369.75 rpm (1500 rpm after the 18/73 first stage) → M1 = 1291 N·m
Ratio and centre distanceτ ≈ 0.245, i = 182 mm
MaterialσH,lim = 1160 MPa, Ke = 35 GPa (steel on steel)
Teeth and modulez1 = 19, z2 = 78, mn = 3.5 mm, helix angle 21.1°
Pitch diametersdp1 = 71.3 mm, dp2 = 292.7 mm
Face widthL = 59.9 mm (φ = L/dp1 = 0.84)
Tangential forceFc = 36 223 N
Lewis stressσL = 311 MPa (y = 0.370, Ψ = 1.017)

The hand solution finds the same pair (z 19/78, mn 3.5, about 21°) and L = 60.6 mm: the 1 % gap comes from reading Φ and Γₜ off the chart. The calculator reproduces 108 checks from 15 exam papers (2015–2024).

What you get

  • Results you can pass, only when you ask, to the next module (for example gears → shaft → bolts).

Limits and next step

Preliminary sizing with the course method: no dynamic, load distribution or lubrication factors, and the allowable Hertz pressure is entered already corrected for the required life. For the final design use ISO 6336.

Questions

Can it check an existing gear pair?
Yes: the "Inverse Check (Max Power W_max)" mode takes module, face width and teeth and returns the power the pair can transmit at the Hertz and Lewis limits.
Does it handle helical gears?
Yes: normal and transverse module, helix angle, and the factors Φ, Ψ and Γₜ from the course chart. With a given centre distance it also finds the helix angle that fits it.
Which Lewis factor does it use?
The y of the course chart as a function of the number of teeth (and of the profile shift), digitised; the value used is written in the report.
Is it the same as ISO 6336?
No. It is the textbook method (Hertz and Lewis) taught in machine design courses, good for preliminary sizing and for exams. ISO 6336 adds dynamic, load distribution and life factors.
Is it free?
Yes, free and without sign-up. Everything runs in your browser.
